## How to make each cell last its full life

Three habits that change your rupee bill, not just your convenience:

- **Open the door at night.** Zinc-air batteries begin working the moment the tab is removed, but power still drains while the aid is on. Cutting power overnight stretches the cell and also lets moisture out.
- **Wait 60 seconds after pulling the tab.** Zinc-air needs a minute of air exposure to reach full voltage. Everything from hollow sound to a silent aid is often just impatience.
- **Buy by blister date, not by brand name.** Fresh stock, proper storage, a reliable seller beats a famous label that sat in a hot bazaar for a year.

## Practical buying and storage in Pakistani conditions

Batteries spoil here faster than in most global markets, and the reason is climate rather than counterfeits alone:

1. **Buy by the month, not by the year.** A case that lasts 12 months in Europe lasts fewer in Karachi heat. Purchase in one-to-three month packs, not bulk, unless you have a genuinely cool, dry cupboard.
2. **Refrigeration does not help.** Condensation from a fridge seal seeds corrosion on the cell contact, so cool-and-dry beats cold-and-damp. A drawer on the side of the house that stays shaded is better than the refrigerator door.
3. **Keep a two-week buffer.** As a rule, when you open the second-last cell in the blister, buy the next pack. Running out mid-month in a city with one pharmacy stock does happen.
4. **Watch the date on the blister.** Zinc-air has a shelf life around 3 to 4 years sealed. Old discount stock is cheap because a part of its life has already been spent sitting in a warehouse.

Buying from a responsible dealer, rather than the cheapest listing, protects against both fakes and stale stock. Ask when the stock arrived and check the printed expiry yourself; responsible sellers answer without offense.

## What a dead cell sounds like versus a dead aid

Discriminating from each other saves you a trip to the dealer that you do not need:

- **Dead battery.** Sound is weak, hollow or absent from a device that was fine this morning, and the swap to a fresh cell cures it instantly.
- **Blocked wax guard or dome.** Sudden drop in one ear, no cure from a battery swap. Cleaning or replacing the wax filter fixes this, and is covered in our [care guide](/blog/how-to-care-for-your-hearing-aids).
- **Moisture in the receiver.** Muffled sound that improves after an hour out of the ear and a night in a drying kit.
- **Real component failure.** Sound stops entirely and everything above has been ruled out. At this point it is a warranty or repair case, and our [repair guide](/blog/hearing-aid-repair-service-pakistan) explains the process and the cost bands.


## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is hearing aid battery price in Pakistan per 6-pack?

Rs 350 to Rs 650 depending on size and seller in 2026. Size 10 (yellow) starts Rs 350 to 450, 312 (brown) Rs 350 to 500, 13 (orange) Rs 400 to 550 and 675 (blue) Rs 450 to 650 per 6-pack.

### Which battery color is which size?

Size 10 is yellow, size 312 brown, size 13 orange, size 675 blue. Count them by tab color, match your aid's battery door size marking, then ask for that color by name. If the packs you get are a different color, they are the wrong size.

### How long does a size 312 hearing aid battery last?

Five to seven days of typical daily wear. Size 10 runs shorter (3 to 5 days), size 13 longer (7 to 10 days), size 675 longest (10 to 14 days). Humidity and heat in Pakistani summer can cut each figure noticeably.

### How much do I spend on batteries per month for two hearing aids?

Roughly Rs 530 to Rs 1,200 per month depending on which size your aids take, with small size 10 cells at the top end and larger size 675 cells costing less per month because they last longer. Real wear with two ears runs 6 to 16 cells monthly.

### Are rechargeable hearing aids cheaper in the long run?

For one ear, roughly at break-even after three to four years of battery savings against the upfront rechargeable premium. For two ears the math stretches. The full cost table and where it makes sense honestly is in the [rechargeable vs battery guide](/blog/rechargeable-vs-battery-hearing-aids).

### Can I use different battery sizes in one hearing aid?

No. Battery size is fixed by the battery door and the aid's power design. A wrong size either does not close, or fails to make contact. If you are unsure what your aid takes, send a photo of the door or battery itself to us on WhatsApp and we will tell you.
