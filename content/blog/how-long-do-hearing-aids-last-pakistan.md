How long do hearing aids last in Pakistan? Figure 5 to 7 years of useful daily life for a well-cared-for digital aid — versus the 7-10 years quoted in cooler, drier markets — because Pakistani heat, humidity and sweat attack the electronics from day one. The range inside that band depends less on the brand than on care habits: a Rs 60,000 Rexton kept in a dry box daily can outlive a Rs 300,000 Phonak worn into a humid Lahore summer without one. This guide covers what actually kills aids, the care that adds years, and the repair-versus-replace math with real rupee figures.

## Why Pakistan Shortens the Clock

Hearing aid manufacturers quote 7-10 years based on temperate-climate use. Three local conditions compress that:

- **Heat.** Sustained 40°C+ summers accelerate degradation of internal components and battery contacts. An aid left on a car dashboard or a window sill in June ages enormously in a single afternoon.
- **Humidity and sweat.** Moisture is the number one killer worldwide, and Pakistani coastal cities (Karachi) and humid plains (Lahore in monsoon) are about as bad as it gets. Sweat from a manual job or a march through August humidity runs down behind the ear straight into the aid's ports. Our [care guide](/blog/how-to-care-for-your-hearing-aids) covers the countermeasures in detail.
- **Dust.** Road dust and construction haze work into microphone ports and battery compartments. A dusty aid hisses and loses clarity before it dies outright.

The good news: every one of these is addressable with habits costing almost nothing. The aids that survive 7+ years in Pakistan aren't luckier — they're dried, cleaned and stored deliberately.

## What Actually Kills Hearing Aids

Ranked by how often they end in a repair invoice at a Pakistani dealer:

| Killer | Effect on lifespan | Prevention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Moisture (sweat, humidity, rain) | Shortens 1–3 years off typical life; causes corrosion, distortion, intermittent shutdowns | Dry box every night (Rs 1,500–4,000), wipe after sweating, remove before showering |
| Dust ingress | Muffled, hissy sound months before failure; mic ports clog | Brush ports daily, store in closed case, avoid windy construction sites |
| Battery corrosion | Kills contacts permanently in weeks | Change cells on time, remove dead cells immediately, never store aids with batteries in long term |
| Earwax buildup | Blocks receivers and wax guards; the most common "dead aid" that isn't dead | Clean daily, replace wax guards every 1–3 months (Rs 300–800) |
| Drops and impacts | Cracked shells, dislodged receivers | Retention cords (Rs 500–2,000), handle over a table not a hard floor |
| Chemical exposure (perfume, hairspray) | Corrodes shell and ports | Spray first, insert aids after |

Notice what's absent: brand. A premium aid dies of moisture exactly like a budget one. The Rs 5,000–15,000 out-of-warranty repair band covers most of the moisture and corrosion cases at a Pakistani service centre — see our full [repair and service guide](/blog/hearing-aid-repair-service-pakistan) for what a repair actually involves and per-fault pricing.

## Daily Habits That Add Years

The routine that separates a 4-year aid from a 7-year one is about ten minutes a day:

1. **Dry box every night, no exceptions.** Rs 1,500–4,000 one-time, and it's the single highest-return accessory money in Pakistan. Electric dehumidifier boxes work better than silica gel but either beats nothing.
2. **Wipe the aid after wearing.** Sweat on the shell is tomorrow's corrosion. A dry cloth and 30 seconds.
3. **Brush the ports daily.** Sound opening and microphone port. Cheap brush from any cleaning kit.
4. **Respect the battery schedule.** Change cells on the rated cycle; remove batteries if storing the aid for more than a few days.
5. **Open the battery door when not in use.** Lets the compartment breathe, relieves contact pressure overnight.
6. **Keep it away from heat sources.** Not on radiators, geysers, dashboards, or direct sun through a window.
7. **Annual service check.** Most dealers do a free or minimal-cost clean-and-check; ask for it. Catches problems before they become repair invoices.

Aids used in genuinely punishing conditions — kitchens, workshops, outdoor labour — deserve an earmold refresh more often and an honest look at a sweatband or protective sleeve.

## Repair or Replace: The Math

When an aid fails, the decision has a real rupee answer rather than a gut feel:

| Situation | Typical cost (PKR) | Verdict |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Under warranty | Rs 0 or minimal | Repair, always. Confirm what the warranty covers first. |
| Minor out-of-warranty fault (mic, receiver, corrosion clean) | 5,000 – 15,000 | Repair if the aid is under ~5 years old and your loss is stable. |
| Major out-of-warranty fault (shell crack + moisture + circuit) | 15,000+ often, or declared unrepairable | Replace if the repair approaches half a new comparable aid's price. |
| Aid is 6+ years old and your hearing has dropped | Varies | Replace — an old aid reprogrammed for a new audiogram is usually the wrong tool. |
| Technology gap (old aid lacks the features you now need) | New aid 15,000 – 550,000+ per ear | Replace on need, not on novelty. |

The clean rule: if a repair costs more than roughly half the price of a comparable new aid, and your aid is past year 5, replace. If it's under year 3 and the repair is under Rs 15,000, repair. Between those poles it's judgement — and it's worth getting a second quote, because repair pricing across Karachi, Lahore and Islamabad varies more than the parts do.

Also separate genuine failure from technology pull. Newer aids — Bluetooth streaming, rechargeable batteries, smarter noise handling — are real improvements and the Rs 50,000+ streamer hardware shows how expensive add-ons get, but an aid that works is not obsolete because a model launched. Upgrade when your hearing, your lifestyle, or the aid's repairability says so, not because the billboards did.

## Warranty Clocks and What They Cover

Standard manufacturer warranty in Pakistan runs 12 to 24 months depending on brand and level, with the paperwork held by the dealer where you bought. What it typically covers: manufacturing faults, internal component failure, one free repair claim per year in some brands. What it never covers: loss, water damage (reads as "user damage" in most claims), cracked shells from drops, wax intrusion, and consumables like earmolds, wax guards and batteries.

Practical points: get the warranty length in writing, register the serial number with the dealer, and check whether the warranty travels across cities — a Chiniot buyer with a Lahore dealer should know where the aid has to go. For repair coverage after the clock runs out, budget the Rs 5,000–15,000 band for minor faults and compare against replacement before committing. And book a hearing test roughly every year if your loss is stable, every 6 months if it's progressing — the [PTA test guide](/blog/pta-hearing-test-price-and-results-pakistan) covers the Rs 1,000–2,000 air+bone test that anchors that decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is 5 years or 10 years the norm for hearing aid lifespan?

Both, depending on where you live and how you care for them. Manufacturer quotes of 7-10 years assume temperate, dry climates and careful users. In Pakistan's heat, humidity and dust, 5-7 years of daily wear is the realistic band — with daily dry-box use and port cleaning pushing toward the upper end.

### Can repairs genuinely extend a hearing aid's life?

Yes, for specific failures. A Rs 5,000–15,000 repair on a 3-4 year old aid that restored full function is good value. What repairs can't do is reverse moisture damage across the whole circuit or upgrade the aid's technology. Repair buys more life on the same platform; it doesn't buy a better platform.

### Does Pakistan's heat really shorten hearing aid life?

Measurably, yes. Sustained high temperatures and coastal or monsoon humidity accelerate corrosion and battery contact damage relative to the temperate-climate conditions behind the 7-10 year manufacturer quote. Aids kept in dry boxes nightly and kept off skin sweat in extreme heat last notably longer here.

### When should I replace my hearing aid instead of repairing it?

Replace when the repair quote is over roughly half the price of a comparable new aid, when the aid is past year 5 and your audiogram has shifted, or when a major fault (shell + moisture + circuit) is quoted at Rs 15,000+ with no clear outcome. Otherwise repair. Don't upgrade on model-year novelty alone.

### Do hearing aid batteries drain faster in Pakistan's summer?

Yes, hot and humid conditions drain zinc-air cells faster than rated — a size 13 rated for 7-10 days may turn in 5-7 in June heat if the compartment breathes poorly. Daily dry-box storage and removing batteries when the aid is stored for extended stretches both help; the [battery price guide](/blog/hearing-aid-battery-price-pakistan) covers per-size pricing, and a battery that dies early repeatedly is usually a humidity or contact issue, not a brand defect.
