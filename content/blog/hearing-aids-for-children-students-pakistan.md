Hearing aids for children in Pakistan start at Rs 60,000–90,000 per ear for a genuine entry-level digital BTE — the practical band for a school-going child — with brand pediatric lines like Phonak Sky running Rs 120,000–300,000 per ear. For a child, this purchase is not cosmetic: the years from birth to school age are the speech and language development window, and an underpowered or badly fitted aid during those years costs vocabulary, classroom participation and confidence that no later upgrade returns. This guide covers the pediatric options actually available here, why BTE with earmold is the standard, and the follow-up rhythm that keeps a child's fitting current as the ear grows.

## Why Children Need a Proper Fit, Not a Bargain Bin

An adult can tolerate an imperfect aid and compensate with context. A child cannot. Two hard reasons:

- **The development window.** Speech and language build on clear auditory input in the first years. A child who hears vowels but misses consonants — the classic high-frequency drop — builds a distorted sound map that shows up years later as reading and spelling difficulties. Paediatric audiology exists to avoid precisely this.
- **No self-report.** A child cannot tell you the aid is too soft, too sharp or whistling. Without objective verification (real-ear measurement or at minimum careful behavioural checks after fitting), a badly set aid can run for months silently underperforming.

The single most common mistake in Pakistani pediatric fittings is buying an adult aid and putting it on a child. Adult cosmetic aids (CIC, IIC) are too small for a child's canal, cannot be handled by parents for battery changes, and often drop out at school. The pediatric standard is a behind-the-ear (BTE) aid with a custom earmold — for reasons the next section makes concrete.

## Why BTE With Earmold Is the Standard for Kids

Three reasons converge on this single configuration:

1. **The ear keeps growing.** A child's ear canal changes shape through childhood, so an in-the-canal aid fitted today won't seat properly in a year. A BTE body stays usable while only the earmold is remade — Rs 2,000–5,000 per remold — a fraction of the cost of replacing a whole canal aid.
2. **Power headroom.** Progressive childhood hearing loss (some types worsen with age) can be accommodated by reprogramming the BTE or swapping to a stronger earmold design, rather than buying a new power aid.
3. **Parental control.** BTE aids are easier for a parent to put on, check, clean and change batteries on. A three-year-old's CIC is impossible to manage honestly.

Standard pediatric accessories add to the per-ear runtime cost and are not optional: retention clips or cords (so the aid stays on during play), dry box (Rs 1,500–4,000 one-time), wax guards (Rs 300–800 per set) and the earmold re-fits. Budget the accessories into the first-year cost, not as an afterthought.

## School Noise and the FM/Roger Question

Classroom acoustics are brutal even for normal-hearing children — a typical Pakistani classroom runs 60–75 dB of ambient noise, and the teacher's voice arrives at a child seated 10 rows back with a signal-to-noise ratio that makes consonants unreadable. Hearing aids alone don't close that gap. The two standard answers:

- **FM / Roger remote microphone systems.** The teacher wears a small microphone that streams their voice directly into the child's aids. Phonak Roger — the market standard — runs as premium add-on hardware in the Rs 50,000+ Bluetooth/streamer category, and education projects in a few Pakistani private schools have trialled it. If your child's school supports it and the budget allows, it's the single biggest classroom upgrade that exists.
- **Seating and teacher briefing.** Free and effective: front-row seating, facing the teacher, teacher facing the class when speaking, verifying the aid batteries before school starts.

Talking to the school before buying the aids, not after, is the order of operations. A teacher who understands what a high-frequency loss means will change how she positions herself; one who doesn't will assume the child is ignoring instructions.

## Battery Care and Young Users

Battery discipline is different with children:

- **Size 13 (orange tab)** and **675 (blue)** are the common pediatric sizes, lasting roughly 7–10 and 10–14 days per cell respectively at Rs 350–650 per 6-pack. See our full [battery sizes explained guide](/blog/hearing-aid-battery-sizes-explained) and [battery price breakdown](/blog/hearing-aid-battery-price-pakistan).
- **Swallowing risk.** Zinc-air batteries are a genuine swallowing hazard for children under 4. Keep spare batteries locked away, and check the compartment door closure daily with very young users. Where money allows, a rechargeable BTE with a locked charging dock removes the daily battery handling entirely — see our [rechargeable vs battery comparison](/blog/rechargeable-vs-battery-hearing-aids).
- **The dead-battery morning.** A child arriving at school with one dead aid loses half the day's language input before anyone notices. Teach older children to check the battery each morning and pack one spare in the school bag; put a spare in the teacher's drawer for younger ones.

## The 6-Month Follow-Up Rhythm

Children's fittings are not set-and-forget. Three clocks tick simultaneously:

- **Ear growth** means the earmold leaks — feedback will be the warning sign — and needs remolding roughly every 6–12 months for younger children, less often as they approach adolescence.
- **Hearing progression** means the audiogram itself should be re-measured approximately every 6 months, especially for any progressive loss type, and the aid reprogrammed if thresholds have shifted.
- **School demands shift** — new classroom, new subjects, more complex vocabulary — and aid programming should be revisited accordingly.

Budget the follow-up appointments into the plan and choose a dealer or clinic near where you actually live, not the cheapest in another city. A Rs 70,000 per ear aid with zero follow-up inside Karachi or Lahore traffic is worse value than a Rs 80,000 per ear aid with the audiologist a rickshaw ride away. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What's the cheapest safe hearing aid option for a child in Pakistan?

An entry-level programmable digital BTE at Rs 60,000–90,000 per ear — Rexton, Unitron or Signia class — with a proper earmold and 6-month follow-up. A&M Pakistani-made units at Rs 8,000–35,000 are genuine for mild loss with careful monitoring, but anything under roughly Rs 20,000 is likely an amplifier, which is unsuitable for a child's development.

### Are rechargeable hearing aids good for children?

Yes, with a caveat. Rechargeables remove the swallowing risk and daily battery handling, which is a real advantage with young children — but you need a charging docking station and power backup, and the upfront cost runs higher. For teenagers, either option works; for under-7s, rechargeables with a locked dock are often the safer choice.

### How often does a child's earmold need replacing?

Every 6–12 months for young children because the ear canal changes shape as they grow, less frequently as they approach their teens. Each remold costs roughly Rs 2,000–5,000. Persistent whistling is the sign that the earmold has started leaking and needs attention.

### Can the school classroom actually support a hearing aid?

Partly. Aids help, but classroom noise (60–75 dB commonly) overwhelms them at distance. The meaningful support is teacher-facing strategies — seat the child front and centre, ask the teacher to face the class when speaking, brief the teacher on the child's specific audiogram — plus, where budget allows, an FM/Roger system that streams the teacher's voice directly into the aids (Rs 50,000+ premium hardware).

### What warranty applies to children's hearing aids?

Standard manufacturer warranty in Pakistan runs 12–24 months, the same band as adult aids, covering manufacturing faults and repairs but not loss or water damage. Pediatric BTE units from Phonak, Oticon or Signia carry the same clock — confirm the warranty length in writing before paying, and note that the earmold is a consumable and not usually covered.
