Bluetooth is the feature people ask about most, and the one most often misunderstood. "Bluetooth hearing aids" can mean three different things, and only one of them lets you take a call without touching your phone.

Here is what each level does, what your phone needs to support, and what the upgrade is worth.

## Three levels of connectivity

### 1. App control

The aid connects to your phone only so you can adjust volume, programs, and treble or bass from an app. No audio passes through. Nearly every modern aid does this, including entry models.

### 2. Audio streaming

Sound from your phone — music, videos, navigation, and the audio side of calls — plays through your hearing aids in stereo. This is genuinely useful and is standard from the mid tier upwards. You still speak into the phone's microphone.

### 3. Hands-free calling

The hearing aid microphones pick up your voice as well, so the phone can stay in your pocket. This is what makes calls feel normal again. It sits at the premium tier and needs a specific combination of phone and hearing aid.

## What your phone supports

Compatibility is the part that trips people up:

- **iPhone (iOS 13 and later):** supports Made-for-iPhone (MFi) hearing aids for both streaming and hands-free calling on compatible models. Hands-free calling over MFi has been reliable for years.
- **Android:** behaviour depends on the phone. Hands-free calling over Bluetooth LE Audio needs Android 14 or later on supported hardware, and a hearing aid that supports the same standard. Older Android phones often stream audio but route your voice through the phone microphone.
- **LE Audio and Auracast:** the current standard allows lower-latency streaming and broadcast audio in public venues. Availability in Pakistan is still limited, and both ends of the link must support it.

Before buying, check the exact model on the manufacturer's compatibility list against your exact phone. "Works with Android" is not specific enough, and the difference between streaming and hands-free is where most disappointment happens.

## What streaming is actually good for

**Phone calls.** The clearest win. Speech goes straight into both ears at a consistent level, which makes the hardest everyday listening task significantly easier.

**Video calls and online meetings.** Same benefit, plus you can hear with your aids rather than holding a headset over them, which usually whistles.

**TV.** A TV streamer accessory sends audio to your aids at a volume that is comfortable for you and unchanged for the rest of the room. This alone resolves a lot of household friction. Cheaper route: a Bluetooth transmitter plugged into the TV, if your aids support standard Bluetooth audio.

**Navigation and voice notes.** Small, but noticeable when you drive.

**Music.** Adequate for casual listening. Hearing aids are tuned for speech, so music sounds thinner than it does through good earphones.

## Battery cost of streaming

Streaming uses noticeably more power than listening through the microphones:

| Use | Effect on runtime |
| :--- | :--- |
| No streaming | Baseline |
| 2 hours of calls per day | Roughly 15–20% less |
| 4+ hours of streaming daily | Roughly 30–40% less |

If you stream for work all day, choose a rechargeable model with a portable charging case, and read [rechargeable vs battery hearing aids](/blog/rechargeable-vs-battery-hearing-aids) before deciding.

## What it costs in Pakistan

Bluetooth audio streaming now appears in mid-range devices, so you do not need a flagship for calls and TV:

- **App control only:** entry tier, from PKR 45,000 per ear
- **Audio streaming:** typically PKR 110,000 – 220,000 per ear
- **Hands-free calling:** typically PKR 250,000 – 400,000 per ear
- **TV streamer accessory:** PKR 25,000 – 60,000, and not always included

## Practical setup tips

- Pair one device at a time, and unpair old phones you no longer use. Stale pairings cause most connection dropouts.
- Keep the aids and phone on the same side of your body; your body blocks the signal.
- If sound is delayed on video, look for a "low latency" or "streaming mode" setting in the fitting software.
- Turn off notifications from apps you do not want announcements from, or every message will arrive in your ears.
- Update your phone before blaming the aids. A large share of Android streaming problems are firmware issues on the phone side.

## Is it worth the upgrade?

If you take calls or watch TV with other people in the room, yes. Those two use cases change daily life more than a marginal improvement in noise handling.

If your listening is mostly face-to-face conversation at home and in the market, put the money into better noise processing instead — that is where the [premium tiers](/blog/hearing-aid-prices-in-pakistan-2026) still earn their price.
