A hearing aid is a computer the size of a bean, worn inside a warm, damp, waxy environment, in a country where the air carries dust for much of the year. That it works at all is remarkable. That it lasts seven years instead of three comes down to routine.

Here is what actually shortens the life of a hearing aid in Pakistan, and what to do about it.

## 1. Clean it dry, every night

Water and hearing aids do not mix, even with an IP68 rating. Wipe the casing with a dry microfibre cloth at the end of the day, then brush the microphone ports and receiver opening with the soft brush that came in the box. Earwax builds up in the same spot on the same schedule, so two minutes nightly beats an hour of scraping later.

## 2. Replace wax guards on schedule, not on failure

The wax guard is a tiny filter that protects the receiver. Once it is blocked, sound drops and users often blame the device. Check it weekly with a magnifier if your eyes allow it, and replace it every four to eight weeks depending on how much wax you produce. Replacement tools are cheap; receivers are not.

## 3. Dry them overnight, properly

Humidity is the bigger enemy than heat. Use a dedicated drying jar with a desiccant capsule, or an electronic drying box if you have one. Open the battery door before putting rechargeable or battery models in the dry box — moisture escapes, batteries last longer, and contacts stop corroding.

Desiccant capsules stop working long before they look different. Replace them as instructed; a jar full of saturated silica is just a humid box with a lid.

## 4. Manage sweat in summer

Sweat is salt water, and salt corrodes contacts. If you work outdoors, exercise, or spend long hours in an unairconditioned car, wipe the devices at midday as well as at night. A simple sweatband helps if you are active. If your aids have a nano-coating claim, treat it as water resistance, not water proofing: do not wear them swimming, and do not leave them in a bathroom.

## 5. Handle them over a soft surface

Most repairs we see start with a drop onto tile or marble. Change batteries, replace wax guards, and clean over a folded towel or while seated at a table. A matte finish makes small devices easier to grip; dry hands make them easier still.

## 6. Keep them out of cars and direct sun

A parked car in Lahore in June reaches temperatures that damage lithium cells and warp earmoulds. Carry a hard case and use it. Do not leave aids on a dashboard, and do not store them in a bathroom cabinet where steam reaches them every morning.

## 7. Get them serviced yearly, even when nothing is wrong

An annual check catches a failing receiver, a hardened tube, or a drifting program before you notice you have stopped hearing well in meetings. Bring the devices in once a year and have the tubing, domes, and filters checked. It is the cheapest maintenance you will ever do.

## Quick reference

| Task | How often |
| :--- | :--- |
| Wipe casing and brush ports | Daily |
| Check and replace wax guard | Every 4–8 weeks |
| Dry in desiccant jar or dry box | Nightly |
| Replace desiccant capsules | As per manufacturer |
| Replace domes or tubing | Every 3–6 months |
| Professional service | Yearly |
