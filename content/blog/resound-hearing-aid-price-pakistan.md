ReSound hearing aid price in Pakistan in 2026 runs from about PKR 45,000 per ear for entry digital models up to PKR 370,000 for the premium flagship tier. ReSound is part of the GN Group, the Danish company behind Jabra, and that lineage shows: these are the hearing aids to shortlist if phone calls, video apps and music streaming matter to you. If your day runs on WhatsApp calls and Zoom meetings, ReSound's connectivity is the strongest argument in the market.

## Why connectivity is ReSound's home ground

GN Group builds both hearing aids and consumer headsets (Jabra), and it pairs them into one ecosystem. Three practical payoffs:

1. **Direct calling.** Higher tiers stream calls straight from iPhone or many Android phones into both aids, so a phone call sounds like a person beside you instead of a distant speaker.
2. **The Smart 3D app.** Volume, program changes, battery status and a find-my-aid function sit in one well-designed app, practical for adult users who want fine control without fiddling with tiny buttons.
3. **MultiMic and accessory range.** For lecture halls or mosque sermons, a companion mic relays the speaker's voice directly into your aids, genuinely useful in Pakistan's large family gatherings.

Local supply is thinner than Signia or Phonak, and that has real consequences:

## The MultiMic and accessories, realistically

ReSound's accessory set in Pakistan is smaller than Phonak's Roger ecosystem, and readers should plan for that rather than assume parity:

- **MultiMic.** A puck-sized microphone you set on a table or clip to a speaker, streaming their voice to your aids. In a mosque, seminar hall or large family dawat, this is the difference between following and losing the thread. Limited local stock means order ahead.
- **Phone Clip / TV streamer.** Older-generation ReSound pairings need a bridge device for non-native streaming phones; newer tiers stream directly. Confirm which you are buying, since the bridge adds PKR 15,000 to 30,000.
- **Remote control puck.** A physical controller for volume and programs if the app is not your habit. Cheaper than replacing the aid over a UI preference.

Ask each of these to be itemised on a written quote. Accessory availability is exactly the area where Pakistan's smaller ReSound supply becomes a real cost rather than a catalogue footnote.

## Before and after purchase for ReSound in Pakistan

Buying well here is more process than product knowledge:

1. **Confirm in writing** the exact model, generation, colour, the warranty duration, and whether fitting and follow-ups are included.
2. **Test with your own phone** at the clinic. Pair, stream one call, one song. If direct streaming is poor on your specific phone, you want to know before the money leaves your hand.
3. **Book the two-week follow-up** at purchase. Fine tuning after the brain re-tunes is where a good relief session becomes a genuinely good fit.
4. **Agree the repair route.** Know the importer service point and expected turnaround; a warranty that is only honoured abroad is of limited value to someone in Faisalabad.

## The ReSound app is the selling point. Test it before payment.

ReSound's phone app handles volume, programs and finding a lost aid, and in Pakistan that last feature is quietly valuable: a mini RIC in a school bag or a bus seat covers the cost of the top tier if it means you find the aid instead of replacing it.

Install the app and pair in the shop, on your actual phone, before paying. If your phone is an older Android or a local brand, connectivity can differ and you want to know that on day zero, not after delivery. Ask the dealer to demo streaming one song and one call; if either stutters on your handset, choose the mid model with a physical button instead.

Budget one extra visit a month in, one later. ReSound programming sessions are short, but the first re-tune around week three is typical across every brand, and it is included free when you buy from an authorized dealer with aftercare in the invoice.
