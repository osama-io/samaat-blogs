## What the warranty actually covers

Almost every genuine hearing aid sold in Pakistan carries a 1 to 2 year manufacturer warranty. Inside that window, two very different things can happen:

| Situation | Covered? | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Loss of power, component failure | Usually yes | Free repair or replacement through the importer |
| Battery door or receiver wire fatigue | Often yes | Wear items vary by brand; ask in writing at purchase |
| Vacuum/dome replacement, cleaning | Often not | Consumables, user cost |
| Moisture damage, sweat corrosion | Rarely | Check each brand's terms before assuming |
| Physical breakage (drop, crush) | No | User damage exclusion |
| Lost aid | No | Replaced at user cost, often at a discount |

The moisture exclusion is the one that surprises Pakistani buyers most: sweat and monsoon humidity cause a substantial share of repairs here and are almost universally excluded. Prevention is cheaper than servicing for this reason, and our [care guide](/blog/how-to-care-for-your-hearing-aids) covers the drying habit that avoids it.

## Signia (and Siemens) service in Pakistan

Signia handles in-country warranty through authorised importers. The process for a warranty claim, as it runs in practice:

1. Bring the device with its warranty card and purchase receipt to the dealer who sold it.
2. The dealer opens a service ticket with the importer, usually a 1 to 2 day approval window.
3. Physical repair turnaround is typically 5 to 7 working days for in-warranty work.
4. Out-of-warranty repairs are quoted after the same diagnostic step, with a repair cost that usually lands between PKR 8,000 and 25,000 depending on the failure.

Keep the warranty card and receipt from day one; without them the importer may treat the job as out-of-warranty even if the calendar is on your side. That paperwork habit also matters when you compare informal channels, covered in the [Daraz, OLX vs dealer](/blog/hearing-aids-daraz-olx-vs-dealer-pakistan) guide, because Marketplace purchases leave you without a ticket to open.

## Repair vs replace: the honest math

Two thresholds decide, roughly:

- **Repair under PKR 25,000 on a mid or premium device** that is younger than 4 years and still comfortable to wear: repair.
- **Repair under PKR 15,000 on an entry or elderly-country-made device** older than 5 years with a hearing need that has increased: usually replace, because the same money buys a newer entry tier with better sound or a fresh warranty.

Edge cases worth considering:

1. **Chronic failure.** If the same device has been in service twice in 12 months, the repair cost is going into a declining asset and replacement usually wins.
2. **Hearing has worsened.** If your audiogram has moved since the fitting, the old device may no longer fit your loss even when repaired. A proper re-test is covered in the [hearing test guide](/blog/hearing-test-in-pakistan-what-to-expect).
3. **Model upgrade.** Mid-tier prices have dropped in rupee terms over the last three years; if your repair estimate approaches the price of a fresh entry tier, look at that first.

## Avoiding copy-device repair bait

This is the single most expensive mistake first-time Pakistani buyers make after buying a copy. A copy device with a failing board or a vacuum-damaged receiver will be handed three quotes in sequence, each "almost there", and each paid cash with no receipt. The total often exceeds a genuine entry tier's price before you admit the device was a copy to begin with. Two markers to read before handing over money:

- Any dealer who diagnoses in one minute and asks cash on the spot, without writing down model, fault and cost.
- Any offer to "upgrade" a copy device into something genuine, which is not technically possible since the core board and microphone and receiver are all different.

Buy genuine, keep the warranty, and pay your repair bills once, on the record.

## A pre-repair checklist that saves real money

Roughly a third of devices sent in for repair are fixed, for free, at home. Run this check before you book a ticket:

1. **Try a fresh battery or a full charge.** A weak cell mimics almost every other fault, from volume loss to complete silence.
2. **Check the battery contacts.** A corroded or shorted contact kills sound; a light scratch with a dry brush is sometimes the whole repair.
3. **Swap in a known-good dome or tube.** A split tube or a blocked dome reads as "the aid is broken" when the aid is fine.
4. **Dry it properly overnight.** A damp receiver sounds identical to a failing one until it dries. Use a drying kit or uncooked rice in a sealed jar for a full night, not a few minutes.
5. **Reset and re-test.** Open the battery door for 30 seconds, restart, and see if the fault clears. Intermittent faults that clear on a restart point to the battery or contact rather than the board.

If the fault survives all five steps, it is a genuine repair case, and the warranty route at the top of this page is the cheapest path available to you.

## What to ask at the service desk

Two minutes of questions at handing-over time prevents the three most common Pakistani repair disputes:

- **"Can you put model, fault and estimated cost on paper?"** Any hesitation is the whole answer; go elsewhere.
- **"Is this repair claimable against my warranty, and how many days?"** For in-warranty Signia, the expected answer is approval in 1 to 2 days and turnaround in 5 to 7.
- **"What happens if you cannot fix it?"** A fair dealer states in advance whether they charge a diagnostic fee, whether they can borrow a loaner device, and what the return path is if a board is unrepairable.

Do not accept a verbal "come back tomorrow". Repair disputes here are almost always argument about the written record that nobody kept, not about technical facts.

## When repair is the wrong answer, spelled out

Three clear exit signals where replacing beats repairing, whatever the quoted repair price:

- **Second failure inside twelve months.** The device's reliability is gone; the next failure is already scheduled in your near future.
- **Audiogram moved materially.** If your hearing has dropped by a step since the fitting, the repaired old device will not fit the new hearing even when working, and the cost is only half-spent.
- **Repair quote above 40% of the replacement price** of an equivalent new device in the same brand tier, with no fresh warranty attached.


## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does hearing aid repair cost in Pakistan?

Out-of-warranty repairs typically run PKR 8,000 to 35,000 depending on brand, fault and city. In-warranty repairs are free through authorised importers, usually with a 5 to 7 working-day turnaround. Get the diagnosis and the cost in writing before you pay.

### How long is a hearing aid warranty in Pakistan?

One to two years from the date of purchase, depending on brand and model tier. Premium tiers often carry the longer term. Ask at purchase and keep the warranty card plus receipt, since both are needed to open a service ticket.

### Does hearing aid warranty cover moisture damage?

Almost never. Sweat and humidity damage is the most common exclusion in Pakistani warranty terms, even though monsoon and coastal climates make it the most common fault. Drying habits from our [care guide](/blog/how-to-care-for-your-hearing-aids) are the real protection.

### How long does a Signia repair take in Pakistan?

In-warranty claims typically run 1 to 2 days for approval at the importer, then 5 to 7 working days for the repair itself. Out-of-warranty jobs are quoted after diagnostic, so budget a week to ten days overall.

### Is it worth repairing a 7-year-old hearing aid?

Usually not if the repair estimate exceeds about 40% of the current price of an equivalent new entry tier, or if the device has failed twice within a year. At that age your hearing may also have shifted, so see our [hearing test guide](/blog/hearing-test-in-pakistan-what-to-expect) before spending on repair.

### Can a copy hearing aid be repaired?

It can be worked on informally, but it cannot be genuinely restored because its core board and microphones are not authentic parts. This kind of repair often ends up repeated and costing more than a genuine entry tier. Avoid copy repairs entirely and read the [disadvantages honest guide](/blog/disadvantages-of-hearing-aids-honest-guide) for the full picture.
