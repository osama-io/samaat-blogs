## Fitting and aftercare for Oticon in Pakistan

An Oticon device is only as good as its fitting, so plan the non-hardware part of the purchase as carefully as the model choice:

- **Demand a real audiogram fit.** The dealer should program from your measured thresholds, not from a "typical old age" preset. If a clinic refuses to show you the room used for testing, treat that as a signal to walk away.
- **Book a two-week follow-up.** First fittings are almost never final: the brain needs a fortnight of listening before the audiologist can fine-tune the mid frequencies sensibly. Ask whether that visit is free.
- **Get spare parts on day one.** Domes, tubes and batteries are cheap and get lost; a power BTE user in Abbottabad should not need to courier AbbRabad spare parts from Lahore every month.
- **Agree the service route in writing.** In-warranty Oticon work is importer-routed in Pakistan, so test that path before you need it, i.e. before the warranty period starts.

## Children and Oticon in Pakistan

For a child with severe to profound loss, XCEED 3 UP is a common recommendation because paediatric fittings need headroom: the child's loss often progresses, and a power BTE has spare gain to re-tune with as the audiogram shifts. Add these three points to any paediatric quote:

1. Ask for a battery lock on the door, a safety feature that stops a child swallowing the 675 cell.
2. Ask for two ear-moulds per year in the quote, since children's canals grow fast and a loose mould causes feedback.
3. Confirm retention clips or a cord option, since a PKR 200,000 aid lost at school is an expensive afternoon.

Paediatric fittings belong in the hands of a paediatric audiologist, not a general dealer. Where a family is too far from a major city, an ordered fitting from the [elderly parents and family guide](/blog/hearing-aid-elderly-parents-guide-pakistan) playbook applies to children too: a named local contact, written warranty terms and one agreed follow-up date before payment.


## Living with an Oticon aid: what the first month looks like

Plan on three visits, not one. First fitting sets gain to your audiogram and you will probably over-count loudness in the first days because everything sounds sharp. The second, a week later, trims the settings once your ear has adapted. The third visit or WhatsApp video call after a month is where small annoyances (whistle on a phone call, muffled TV) get fixed with a program change.

Daily care is the same as any brand: dry storage at night, wax guard every two months, keep state away from bathrooms. What is different with Oticon is the software: a full programming session needs the Oticon-specific fitting software, so when you compare dealers, ask who performs reprogramming after six months. That question separates a real dealer from a first-copy reseller, and it matters more in Pakistan than it does in Europe.

If you lose one aid out of warranty, replacing a single unit is possible; bring the survivor in so the fitter can match settings.
